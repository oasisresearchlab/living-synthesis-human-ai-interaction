Claude:
> The session read Zheng (2025), the CHI paper on AgentBuilder, a no-code tool that lets library professionals build and test their own conversational agents. Joel Chan framed the paper's evaluation as a second formative study rather than an efficacy test. The discussion centered on reframing "non-AI experts" as people with different expertise (vernacular software development vs. vibe coding), whether one-off response edits change the agent at all, whether chain-of-thought can provide source transparency, and the knowledge-upload failures, which led to a broader question about eliciting knowledge that isn't in machine-readable documents. LLM-simulated personas were contested: Joel Chan's stereotyping hypothesis vs. S05's view that behaviorally specific personas are better. Housekeeping included a related study of machine-translation-mediated conversation (shared in the seminar), on agency and its costs for non-native speakers using machine translation.

### Questions
- [[QUE - How might we elicit the knowledge domain experts need to steer LLM agents when some of it is in inaccessible formats, unwritten, or hard to verbalize?]]
- [[QUE - How would end-user tools for creating conversational agents work for people who are neither AI experts nor domain experts, or who have no prior LLM experience?]]
- [[QUE - How well do current LLM pipelines extract knowledge from human-oriented documents like heavily formatted PDFs and flyers, and do existing benchmarks capture this?]]
- [[QUE - Does demonstrating a specific AI tool in formative interviews steer participants toward that technology as the solution?]]

### Claims
- [[CLM - framing target users as non-AI experts obscures the domain expertise they bring, which systems should help align models with]]
- [[CLM - one-off edits to an LLM agent's responses do not help users design the agent unless the edits persist into its future behavior]]
- [[CLM - exposing chain-of-thought traces is a weak form of source transparency because the traces may not faithfully reflect the model's actual reasoning]]
- [[CLM - the knowledge domain experts need to steer an LLM agent is often not available as machine-readable documents, because it is in inaccessible formats, unwritten, or hard to verbalize]]
- [[CLM - having domain experts actually design a system elicits tacit expertise that interviews alone are unlikely to surface]]
- [[CLM - persona descriptions with specific behavioral characteristics may be less prone to stereotyping than category labels when LLMs simulate users]]
- [[CLM - the convergence of commercial LLM agent builders on role prompts and knowledge upload retroactively validates these as core end-user customization primitives]]
- [[CLM - vibe coding and vernacular software development are orthogonal framings - the first discounts expertise and precision, the second centers the developer's domain expertise]]
- [[CLM - in emerging application domains a prototype evaluation study can function as further requirements elicitation rather than as a test of efficacy]]
- [[CLM - interaction designs that increase user agency by requiring more resource involvement can impose costs on the user's primary task]]
