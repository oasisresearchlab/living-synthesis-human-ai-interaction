Claude:
> SP-25 Week 11 read Chung & Kreminski (2024) on Patchview ([[@chungPatchviewLLMpoweredWorldbuilding2024]]). Joel Chan steered the group through problem, mechanism, and evidence in order. The richest thread was the paper's idea of "verbally elusive" intent. The group pushed it toward new settings: English language learners, oral histories missing from training data, therapy, and interpretive qualitative analysis as "worldbuilding" (contrasted with [[ART - LLOOM]]'s taxonomizing). Two things were contested: whether a short think-aloud lab study was the right evaluation, and whether spatial steering can replace prompts or only complements them when users need specificity. The group also noted the weak few-shot backend and the lack of support for time.

### Claims
- [[CLM - some user intentions for generative models are verbally elusive and poorly served by natural-language prompts]]
- [[CLM - natural-language prompts can fail to convey intent about content that is underrepresented in a model's training data, even when users can verbalize it]]
- [[CLM - direct manipulation suits rough, exploratory steering of generative models, while code or natural language is preferred when users need precise, specific control]]
- [[CLM - interpretive qualitative analysis resembles worldbuilding because its goal is to make a social world accessible to others, not to count instances of predefined categories]]
- [[CLM - taxonomy-producing LLM analysis tools fit measurement-oriented qualitative paradigms better than narrative or interpretive ones]]
- [[CLM - LLM-generated qualitative codes are more useful as an adjustable starting point, like a colleague's open codes, than as a finished analysis]]
- [[CLM - HCI systems papers usually trade depth of evaluation for depth of design, because building a system and running a full evaluation are each a lot of work]]
- [[CLM - think-aloud lab sessions capture in-the-moment confusion with a prototype that longer in-situ use tends not to surface, at the cost of ecological validity]]

### Evidence
- [[EVD - worldbuilders answered landscape (distribution) questions about a generated world faster and more accurately with Patchview than with a list-only baseline - @chungPatchviewLLMpoweredWorldbuilding2024]]
- [[EVD - worldbuilders answered comparison questions about a generated world at about the same speed and accuracy with Patchview as with a list-only baseline - @chungPatchviewLLMpoweredWorldbuilding2024]]
- [[EVD - worldbuilders found steering by repositioning elements in Patchview easy and natural, but felt natural-language prompts were better for specific requests - @chungPatchviewLLMpoweredWorldbuilding2024]]
- [[EVD - worldbuilders used Patchview to construct their own complex concept views and multi-view structures - @chungPatchviewLLMpoweredWorldbuilding2024]]
- [[EVD - in Patchview, recognition errors fell as users added examples but steering errors did not - @chungPatchviewLLMpoweredWorldbuilding2024]]
- [[EVD - worldbuilders using Patchview wanted to represent how characters and their alignments change over time - @chungPatchviewLLMpoweredWorldbuilding2024]]
- [[EVD - worldbuilders using Patchview reported missing features needed for real workflows, such as filtering the element list and consistently updating characters - @chungPatchviewLLMpoweredWorldbuilding2024]]
