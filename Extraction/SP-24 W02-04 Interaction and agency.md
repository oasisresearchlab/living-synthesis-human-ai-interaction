Claude:
> This note covers the SP-24 seminar's welcome and meta notes (W01 opening questions), then three readings: ImageSense ([[@kochImageSenseIntelligentCollaborative2020]], W02), Graphologue ([[@jiangGraphologueExploringLarge2023]], W03), and Heer's "Agency plus automation" ([[@heerAgencyAutomationDesigning2019]], W04). The running thread is sharing control and agency with intelligent assistance through richer interaction media than linear chat (canvases, graphs, shared representations, predictive interaction). The most contested point was that "agency" is never defined: the readings treat it as control, or as the share of actions taken by the user. S05 argued that designer-afforded control is not user agency, and the group pulled in definitions from linguistics, sociology, psychology, and STS. Other threads were the risk that structured LLM output increases overreliance, and the effects of AI suggestions on homogenization and priming, at both individual and collective levels.

### Claims

- [[CLM - HCI systems research often leaves agency undertheorized, operationalizing it as share of actions or self-reported control]]
- [[CLM - full control is not necessarily the desirable form of agency in creative tasks]]
- [[CLM - research that pairs system design with a user study risks equating user agency with the control that designers choose to afford]]
- [[CLM - linear chat is a suboptimal interaction medium for people to control intelligent assistance in information-seeking tasks]]
- [[CLM - structuring LLM output into official-looking diagrams can mask underlying errors and increase unwarranted reliance]]
- [[CLM - AI assistance can homogenize creative output at the collective level even when no effect is visible at the individual level]]
- [[CLM - fully pre-populated AI suggestions can prime users toward the AI output, reducing their initiative and independent judgment]]
