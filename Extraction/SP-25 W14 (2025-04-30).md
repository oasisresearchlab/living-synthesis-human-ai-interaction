Claude:
> The last SP-25 reading: İnan et al. (2025), "Better Slow than Sorry: Introducing Positive Friction for Reliable Dialogue Systems" ([[@inanBetterSlowSorry2025]]). The class read positive friction as conversational grounding and repair (Clark and Wilkes-Gibbs), and valued the paper's taxonomy for making the idea testable. They then contested almost everything else: whether the problem exists for current chatbots, the "expert" annotators and their low agreement, the simulated users, and whether a context-free table of friction moves invites reward hacking. A long tangent on RLHF, sycophancy, and reward hacking framed why friction matters. A recurring thread was that friction's value depends on the user's task framing and expertise, and should be judged over the whole conversation.

### Claims
- [[CLM - positive friction is better understood as a dialogue agent initiating collaborative effort to coordinate meaning than as slowing the interaction down]]
- [[CLM - the cost of friction in a dialogue should be judged over the whole conversation rather than per turn, because up-front specification and later repair trade off]]
- [[CLM - positive friction likely helps users with weak mental models of their task but is a net negative for experts who can fully specify what they want]]
- [[CLM - a context-free typology of friction utterances invites reward-hacked models that produce friction-like utterances without acting on them]]
- [[CLM - translating a theory-derived concept like positive friction into a typology of concrete dialogue moves makes its effects testable and trainable]]
- [[CLM - chatbot sycophancy is a form of reward hacking in which people-pleasing becomes a shortcut to annotator approval]]
- [[CLM - chatbot sycophancy parallels engagement-optimizing social media feeds, since both optimize a proxy reward that diverges from user values]]
- [[CLM - LLM-simulated users are defensible for theorizing only when clearly distinguished from empirical observation of the people one wants to make claims about]]
- [[CLM - who annotators are and what they know shapes model evaluations and training signals, yet NLP papers often treat annotators as black boxes]]
- [[CLM - NLP and ML venue norms that reward graphs and error bars push researchers toward simulated rather than qualitative human data]]
- [[CLM - harmful chatbot behaviors like sycophancy surface when people adopt chatbots for uses, like therapy, that diverge from how the models were trained and evaluated]]

## Bears on existing nodes
Claude:
> - [[PTN - common ground]]: this session is where the node came from. The new nodes add the specific mechanism (repair, least collaborative effort) and the critique that a context-free typology loses what common-ground theory says about prior context (Joel Chan, SP-25 W14, [00:37:10]).
> - [[THE - conversational repair]]: positive friction was read as repair, "to me it's just Clark ... it's doing repair" (SP-25 W14, [00:34:09]). The session adds other-initiated repair and the idea of turning Clark and Wilkes-Gibbs's tables into LLM policy rules.
> - [[QUE - how-should the way a human repair with a chatbot differ from how they would repair with a person?]]: S05 extended this: users may not see task-oriented agents as collaborators, so they may calibrate grounding effort differently (S05, Connector, SP-25 W14). Captured as a sibling question rather than a duplicate.
> - [[QUE - how-should chatbot be designed to converse in a way that resembles how humans coordinate meaning or just be designed as a bot that follows rule]]: a student asked whether dialogue models can be simplified relative to human conversation theories when one interlocutor is a machine that serves the other; Joel Chan's intuition was that you "probably can't simplify the model" (SP-25 W14, [00:37:57]-[00:38:46]). Not made a separate node.
> - [[ART - Multi-Domain Wizard-of-Oz dataset (MultiWOZ)]]: S11 and Joel Chan stressed that MultiWOZ is crowdsourced, template-driven, simulated human-human dialogue, not naturalistic conversation (S11, Connector; Joel Chan, SP-25 W14, [00:44:03]).
> - [[ART - TEACh dataset]] and [[@sunSimulatingUserSatisfaction2021]]: used as the data behind the paper's annotations and satisfaction experiment; see the EVD nodes above.
> - [[PTN - Seamful XAI]]: Joel Chan connected positive friction to seamful design (Connector thread, SP-25 W14).
> - [[CLM - If-then statements support transparent and interpretable LLM policy formulation]]: Joel Chan noted that Clark's framework already reads like if-then policy rules (SP-25 W14, [00:36:49]).
> - [[PTN - simulated user conversations with LLM-generated personas for testing conversational agents]] (created in parallel by another session): narrower than [[PTN - LLM-simulated human subjects]], which covers simulation as a research method (homo silicus, CHI 2025 panel). The two should probably be linked or merged on review.
