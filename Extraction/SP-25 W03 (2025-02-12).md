Claude:
> The session read Wu, Terry & Cai (2022), AI Chains ([[@wuAIChainsTransparent2022]]). Discussion moved quickly from what the paper shows to whether it still matters. Participants argued that 2025 models with built-in chain-of-thought can do the paper's tasks in one prompt. They also questioned whether a linear chain fits interdependent, nonlinear tasks; Joel Chan tied this to crowdsourcing's retreat from fixed micro-task workflows. The counter-thread, which Joel Chan framed as a hypothesis, was that the value lies less in the chain than in *articulating the subtasks*, which helps people learn, control, and evaluate the task. Support came from Tutor CoPilot, Stepwise, a related study of multilingual co-writing (shared in the seminar), and a long tangent on translation (honorifics, gender, formality, writer- vs. reader-responsible discourse), which showed how much a "simple" task hides.

### Claims
- [[CLM - articulating the subtasks of a task can help people learn, control, and evaluate the work even when it does not improve model performance]]
- [[CLM - micro-task workflow decomposition breaks down for complex work with many interdependencies that requires open-ended adaptation]]
- [[CLM - LLM chains may be most valuable for novices with fuzzy mental models of a task, because the chain can encode an expert's task model]]
- [[CLM - the value added by explicit LLM chaining shrinks as base models internalize chain-of-thought reasoning]]
- [[CLM - a visual representation of the task model can add value even when a model can do the task end to end, because users cannot see how a text-only model reaches its answer]]
- [[CLM - task decomposition is an alternative to stuffing a large context window, because it forces deliberate choices about what context each step needs]]
- [[CLM - tasks that look simple and linear, such as translation, often hide many nuanced dimensions that become visible only when people articulate the subtasks]]
- [[CLM - letting users specify tone or formality in a translation tool can prompt non-proficient users to reflect on what target-language output they need]]
- [[CLM - the controllability benefits of LLM chaining depend on the user's ability to decompose the problem well]]
