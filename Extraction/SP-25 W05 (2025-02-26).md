Claude:
> The session read Petridis et al. (2024), ConstitutionMaker ([[@petridisConstitutionMakerInteractivelyCritiquing2024]]). About half of the discussion explained how prompt-based steering works: the "improv game" model of LLMs, system prompt vs. truncated history, and inference-time prompting vs. fine-tuning vs. Constitutional AI as a training scheme. The rest critiqued the paper: its untested assumption that LLMs follow instructions, its self-report-only evidence of effectiveness, the conflicting principles that participants wrote even when working alone, and the ambiguity over whether the tool is for end users or designers. Side threads covered agency vs. outcome trade-offs in feedback interfaces, positive feedback, LLM-as-judge, and EvalGen's criteria drift.

### Claims
- [[CLM - prompt-based steering tools rest on the largely unvalidated assumption that LLMs reliably follow natural-language instructions in their prompts]]
- [[CLM - inference-time steering through the prompt conditions an LLM without changing its weights, so its influence is weak relative to fine-tuning on large text collections]]
- [[CLM - the levers for human control in human-AI interaction depend on the model class - glass-box models expose editable components, while LLMs mainly expose the prompt]]
- [[CLM - self-reported ease of expressing intent does not show that a steering tool made the model behave as intended]]
- [[CLM - single-item self-report questions have unknown reliability and construct validity compared with validated multi-item instruments like NASA-TLX]]
- [[CLM - users steering an LLM through many fine-grained natural-language principles will author principles that conflict with each other, even working alone]]
- [[CLM - hard-coding principle generation to be specific and conditional may increase conflicts among principles]]
- [[CLM - the absence of edits in an edit-based AI feedback interface is ambiguous between user satisfaction and inability or unwillingness to edit]]
- [[CLM - supporting positive feedback lets users reinforce desired AI behaviors rather than only steering away from bad ones]]
- [[CLM - whether a person is pursuing their own task or designing an AI for others shapes how much effort they will invest in giving feedback to steer it]]
- [[CLM - in LLM chat, content in the system prompt persists across turns while earlier conversational turns are truncated once the context window fills]]
- [[CLM - calling LLM errors hallucinations is misleading because the model always generates likely next tokens and has no built-in step that verifies correctness]]
- [[CLM - a mental model of LLMs as an improv game conditioned on the prompt helps users anticipate what LLMs will and will not do well]]
- [[CLM - prompt engineering remains a dark art without a principled understanding of how models condition their outputs on prompts]]
