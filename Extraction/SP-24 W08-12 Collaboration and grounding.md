Claude:
> Four SP-24 sessions in the "Collaboration / teaming / grounding" unit: W08 Geiger (2017) on bots and Wikipedia's organizational culture; W10 Feuston and Brubaker (2021) on AI in qualitative analysis; W11 Gupta et al. on COHUMAIN and the transactive systems model of collective intelligence; W12 Bennett et al. (2023) on how HCI understands agency and autonomy. Main threads: opacity that is organizational rather than technical, and how bots reshape newcomer socialization; which stage of qualitative analysis AI may touch without taking the researcher "out of the instrumentation"; whether a cognitive-architecture approach to human-AI collectives is premature, and whether CSCW concepts (grounding, repair) carry over; and agency as an umbrella term vs. a boundary object, including whether more individual agency is always good. Contested points: S11 doubted the COHUMAIN agenda's readiness from an NLP vantage; S13 pushed back on overuse of "boundary object"; Joel Chan questioned why QualiGPT cites Feuston and Brubaker.

- [[CLM - algorithmic audits work better as ongoing public practices around matters of concern than as one-time certifications of matters of fact]]
- [[CLM - whether qualitative researchers accept AI assistance depends on the stage of analysis - early coding is where researchers come to know their data, so AI help there threatens the analysis]]
- [[CLM - probing LLMs one isolated failure case at a time cannot tell us how those failures affect people at the level of sociotechnical systems]]
