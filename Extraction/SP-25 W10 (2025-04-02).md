Claude:
> Week 10 read Anthropic's blog post evaluating feature steering with sparse autoencoder (SAE) features as a way to reduce social biases ([[@durmusEvaluatingFeatureSteering2024]]), held over Zoom. The discussion centered on the off-target finding: features that are monosemantic in what they represent still have entangled effects when steered. Two disputes ran through the session. One was whether bias can be a context-free knob inside the model at all. The other was how to read research from a lab that sells the product: one participant called it trust-building "cosplay", while Joel Chan said the authors are upfront that steering is not production-ready. The session also compared steering with prompt engineering (more accessible vs. more predictable), and imagined feature knobs as UI controls for creative writing.

### Claims
- [[CLM - SAE features that are monosemantic in what they represent are not independent when used as steering knobs, because concepts are entangled in the model and in the world]]
- [[CLM - treating social bias as a context-free knob inside the model ignores that whether an output is biased depends on its context of use]]
- [[CLM - feature steering is not yet reliable enough to give users precise, production-ready control over model behavior]]
- [[CLM - feature steering may suit stylistic and creative control, where unexpected interactions are tolerable, better than control of contextual social constructs like bias]]
- [[CLM - prompting is a more accessible steering mechanism than feature steering for non-technical users, but feature steering may offer more predictable dose-response control]]
- [[CLM - exposing model features as user-facing knobs requires users to understand both what a feature means and how output will change as its activation is scaled]]
- [[CLM - incremental interpretability results that give humans granular control over LLM internals are a meaningful contribution even when the experiments are simple]]
