Claude:
> The session read Lam et al. (2024), *AI Policy Projector* ([[@lamAIPolicyProjector2024]]), after about 12 minutes of spring-break synthesis homework logistics. Joel Chan framed the paper as the "backward pass" (figuring out what the policy should be) in contrast to [[PTN - Constitutional AI]]'s "forward pass" (getting models to follow policy). He then walked through the secret sauce: cases, concepts, and if-then policies. The most contested threads were: how much the UMAP map layout can be trusted and what it distorts; what "AI policy" and "policy gap" mean; and the single-user design versus the collaborative, governance-laden reality of policy making (consensus, edit rights, voting bias, case law). The offline steering results (with the graphic-violence plateau from 3 to 5 examples) and the lack of support for reasoning about interactions between policies came up near the end.

### Claims
- [[CLM - designing good LLM policies requires iterating between concrete cases of model behavior and abstract principles, not writing rules from principles alone]]
- [[CLM - LLM policy, as a specification of desired model behavior, is a narrow subset of AI governance]]
- [[CLM - the de facto policies of deployed chatbots are set implicitly through example-level preference judgments rather than explicit, inspectable principles]]
- [[CLM - map-like projections of high-dimensional embeddings need to communicate what their layout distorts, not just that information is lost]]
- [[CLM - adding user-defined concept embeddings to whole-case sentence embeddings pulls a 2D case map toward those concepts, which whole-case embeddings alone dilute]]
- [[CLM - human-AI steering tools are mostly designed and evaluated for a single technically skilled user, though real policy making is collaborative]]
- [[CLM - LLM policy design tools treat cases as data examples and could borrow from how case law weighs, cites, and compares precedents]]
- [[CLM - policy design tools that rely on an aligned LLM to summarize or classify harmful cases may be undermined by that LLM's own safety refusals]]
- [[CLM - grounding policy authoring in a broad landscape of cases may help policy designers avoid cherry-picked cases and cover more failure modes]]
- [[CLM - the same natural-language LLM policies can be enforced at the prompt level or by fine-tuning model weights, so policy design tools can be decoupled from the enforcement mechanism]]
