Claude:
> The session read Arawjo et al. (2024) on ChainForge, a visual toolkit for prompt engineering and LLM hypothesis testing, plus announcements on direct manipulation vs. programming (Ma'ayan et al. 2020) and vs. agents (Shneiderman–Maes debate). The main threads were whether ChainForge counts as direct manipulation (Joel argued it is visual programming, not DM), how its "secret sauce" bridges exploratory side-by-side comparison and systematic testing, and why users rarely made that bridge. The discussion converged on scientific thinking (operationalizing constructs as metrics) as a precondition the tool doesn't scaffold, and on the value of open-ended evaluation for surfacing such preconditions. A tangent on English vs. Indonesian prompting led to contested points on language bias and what "low-resource" means.

### Claims
- [[CLM - exploratory side-by-side comparison of LLM outputs generates the hypotheses that systematic tests need, and systematic tests turn those hypotheses into more than anecdotes]]
- [[CLM - translating an intuitive construct into a trustworthy metric is a scientific-thinking meta-skill that is a precondition for benefiting from systematic LLM testing tools]]
- [[CLM - without skill in operationalizing constructs, systematic LLM testing tools can produce the appearance of systematic testing without its substance]]
- [[CLM - LLM applications are a natural fit for variation theory because their outputs can be cheaply resampled across prompts, parameters, and models]]
- [[CLM - a visual programming interface is not direct manipulation unless users directly edit the object of their core task]]
- [[CLM - open-ended evaluations with participants outside the designers' own circle can surface preconditions for successful tool use that controlled lab studies miss]]
- [[CLM - explore-then-systematically-test modes in LLM prompt engineering mirror well-known opportunistic versus systematic modes in design cognition and sensemaking]]
- [[CLM - interface agents should complement rather than replace direct manipulation, and users must be able to understand and bypass them]]
- [[CLM - a language's low-resource status in NLP reflects how much training text exists relative to current methods, not the language's value]]
