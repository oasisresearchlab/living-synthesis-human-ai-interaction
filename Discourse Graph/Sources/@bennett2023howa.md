## Discussion notes
Claude:
> Annotated by S13 (notes dated April 2nd, 2024, during the SP-24 seminar). The paper asks "How does HCI Understand Human Agency and Autonomy?" by "reviewing 30 years of HCI research on autonomy and agency to identify current understandings, open issues, and future directions" (Bennett et al., 2023, p. 1).
>
> **What the review did**
> - Searched the ACM Digital Library for CHI publications (May 21st, 2022), yielding 207 full papers; papers with no discussion of human agency and autonomy were excluded, leaving 161 papers over 32 years (pp. 1, 3-4).
> - Built an analysis rubric via thematic analysis of a subset of 77 papers, coded by the first author in NVivo, with "peer validation ... through regular meetings among the co-authors" (pp. 3-4).
>
> **Key findings**
> - Agency and autonomy currently figure "not as effective boundary objects in the HCI landscape, but as vague umbrella constructs ... subsuming a surprising diversity of understandings and theoretical underpinnings" (p. 2). The terms are "often used interchangeably", and "few works give specific definitions" (p. 2): "Only one third of papers in our corpus (n=58) gave explicit definitions of autonomy or agency" (p. 6).
> - Etymology: autonomy from autos + nomos, "self-governance"; agency from agere, "to set in motion, drive forward; to do, perform", emphasizing self-causality. Distinct but "tightly entangled" (p. 2).
> - Why they are valued: n = 21 papers treat them as having "intrinsic value"; the majority (n=90) locate their value in benefits for users, e.g., life satisfaction, overcoming barriers (ageing, disability, resources), buffering stress, enhanced learning, and privacy ("autonomy is like the 'muscle' of privacy") (pp. 4-5).
> - Autonomy can cut both ways: workers given "autonomy in task scheduling and decision making" bore "additional stress, responsibility, unaccounted labour, and skipped toilet breaks" (p. 5). Three papers describe "paradoxes" where restricting agency or autonomy seemed to increase it (p. 8).
> - "Very few papers explicitly engaged with existing theoretical accounts." Self-Determination Theory (incl. METUX) was most frequent (n=17); others include Actor Network Theory, value-sensitive design, social anarchism, and Kantian philosophy (p. 6).
> - Sense-of-agency (the "implicit sense of authorship of an action", operationalized via intentional binding) can be manipulated through sense-of-ownership, haptic feedback, or timing, and users can feel agency where they have no causal influence (pp. 7-8).
> - Four aspects that help clarify agency and autonomy: "1. issues of self-causality and personal identity; 2. the experience and material expression of autonomy and agency; 3. particular timescales; and 4. emphasis on independence and interdependence" (p. 12).
> - Ethical challenges: who benefits from the user's agency; "delegation of worker decision-making to AI reduced attentiveness and engagement, leading to poorer task outcomes" (p. 11); and autonomy in self-construction (p. 12).
> - Recommendations: explicitly state definitions; use the four aspects; link specific aspects to outcomes; do more integrative work across HCI communities (p. 13).
> - On boundary objects: they need interpretive flexibility but also robustness; they rely on "refinement of the concepts to address local concerns" plus "coordination of these local understandings with the concept's wider identity" (p. 9).
>
> **Critiques and questions (S13)**
> - Clarity: a concept-analysis paper like this should use nested-set or Venn representations to show relationships between codes. The results mostly list papers and findings; integration seems missing.
> - Opportunity for a more interdisciplinary review drawing on psychology and philosophy (Self-Determination Theory appears early).
> - On "boundary objects": "I'm beginning to think that this term is being overused."
> - Responding to Aristotle's autarkeia (p. 2): "Are autonomy and agency ultimate goods or good as means to ends? What if you can self-govern and have agency, but are not successful in goal fulfillment? That, to me, indicates the autonomy and agency as means to higher ends like wellbeing or eudaimonia."
