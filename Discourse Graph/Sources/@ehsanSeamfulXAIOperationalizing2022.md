## Discussion notes
Claude:
> **What it proposes.** Brought in by S06 as 🏺 Archaeologist in SP-24 (as a later "after" paper). Mistakes in AI systems are inevitable, arising from both technical limitations and sociotechnical gaps. Black-boxing AI can make the experience seamless, but hiding the **seams** risks disempowering users from mitigating fallout from AI mistakes. The paper introduces [[PTN - Seamful XAI]]: strategically revealing sociotechnical and infrastructural mismatches, by (1) transferring the concept of "seams" to AI and (2) developing a design process that **helps stakeholders design with seams**, to augment explainability and user agency.

Claude:
> **Evidence.** The process was explored with 43 AI practitioners and users in a scenario-based co-design activity informed by real-world use cases.

Claude:
> **Where it sits in the design space.** In SP-25, Joel Chan used this paper as an example of what the interaction-focused reading list ([[QUE - What is the design space of --interaction-oriented-- approaches to Human-Centered AI?]]) leaves out: "larger policy- or sociotechnically-oriented approaches to human-centered AI, such as accountability/risk frameworks, audits, etc., or changes in the way that systems are *designed* in the first place."
