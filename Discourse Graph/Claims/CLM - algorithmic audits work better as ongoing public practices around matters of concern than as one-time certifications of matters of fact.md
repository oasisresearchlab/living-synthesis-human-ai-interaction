> - Brought in by S06 (Archaeologist, SP-24 W08) as Geiger's recent follow-on work, moving "from understanding algorithmic systems to giving design suggestions": [[@geigerMakingAlgorithmsPublic2024]].
> - From its abstract (quoted by S06): audits are "typically envisioned as settling 'matters-of-fact' about how opaque algorithmic systems behave: definitive declarations that (de)certify a system. However, there is little consensus about the decisions to be automated or about the institutions automating them." The authors "reposition algorithmic auditing as an ongoing and ever-changing practice around 'matters-of-concern'", building infrastructures for the public to engage in "open-ended democratic understanding, contestation, and problem solving", about the institutions and power structures deploying algorithms as well as the algorithms. Scoping an audit is "contestable, negotiable, and political".
> - Not discussed further in the notes, so this is the paper's claim more than the seminar's.
