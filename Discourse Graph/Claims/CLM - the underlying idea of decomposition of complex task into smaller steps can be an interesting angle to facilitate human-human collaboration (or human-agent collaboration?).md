## Grounds
Claude:
> Proposed by S05 (🍜 Academic Researcher role) in the SP-25 seminar discussion of LLM chaining ([[@wuAIChainsTransparent2022]]), as a slight departure from the paper's focus on transparency and control.
> - Grounding from a related study of multilingual co-writing (shared in the seminar), on co-writing among people from different language backgrounds: writing tasks are *implicitly* composed of smaller steps (e.g., ideating content, producing words to express ideas). When participants treated "writing" as a single task, they tended to think people with low language proficiency contributed almost nothing. Once "writing" was broken into subcomponents, it became clear that people could contribute to ideation regardless of proficiency, but needed a "protected" space to do so without worrying about wording. (SP-25)
> - This connects to [[PTN - Articulation Work]].
> - For human-AI collaboration, S05 linked it to the authors' future work on [[QUE - how can end user effectively author their own LLM chains?]]: humans may break down a task one way and the model another. Neither is necessarily better, but the divergence can be a starting point for negotiation, both to improve human task performance and to give feedback to the model. (S05, SP-25)

Possible studies: (1) an ethnography-style study of how a team uses a chaining tool to coordinate, or (2) an experiment comparing team task planning with and without a chaining tool. (S05, SP-25)
