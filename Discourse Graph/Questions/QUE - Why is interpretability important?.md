## Context
Claude:
> Raised by S13 as 👩🏽‍🔬 Distiller in the SP-24 seminar session on interpretability, drawing on Lipton's account ([[@liptonMythosModelInterpretability2017]]). Related: [[QUE - What is interpretability?]].
> - **Mismatch between objectives and real-world costs.** Lipton: "The demand for interpretability arises when there is a **mismatch between the formal objectives of supervised learning (test set predictive performance) and the real world costs costs in a deployment setting**."
> - **Contestability.** The EU position: "individuals affected by algorithmic decisions have a right to explanation...**algorithmic decisions should be contestable.**" This requires that systems (i) "present **clear reasoning based on falsifiable propositions**" and (ii) "offer some natural way of **contesting these propositions and modifying the decisions** appropriately if they are falsified."

## Other Notes
Claude:
> Seminar responses on what contestability could look like:
> - Weighing factors, so a user can contest the weights. When there are multiple "correct" outputs, the question is which option fits the nuance of this context; show how outputs differ from each other and let the user decide. (S05, SP-24)
> - The user checks whether the claimed factors are actually used. Contrastive rationales help here: "this region → tumor likely, but it's not something else", as in Contrastive Explanations for Model Interpretability (https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01378). (S11, SP-24)

> - On "falsifiable propositions": this seems to come from Popperian philosophy of science, and suggests applying philosophy-of-science ideas (explanation, mechanisms) to enrich our understanding of interpretability.
