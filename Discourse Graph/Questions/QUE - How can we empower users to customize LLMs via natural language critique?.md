Claude:
> Key question of [[@petridisConstitutionMakerInteractivelyCritiquing2024]], discussed in the SP-25 seminar (Distiller notes).
> - Design pattern: [[PTN - interactive natural language critique]]; specifically, "compile" critiques so they persist as **principles** (like in a Constitution).
> ![[Boxohj93Uo.png]]
> ![[GVIighJwRn.png]]
> - Formative study with 8 industry professionals: 25 minutes writing principles, then a chat about their experience, yielding 79 principles.
> ![[pP4Wyx7HdE.png]]
> ![[huZ557XEg6.png]]
> - Principle classification: conditional vs. unconditional; scope of conditional context (e.g., entire history, last response, next action); scope of conditional action (single vs. multiple conversation turns).
> ![[Ad_WGItDjx.png]]
> ![[2Qczduztoe.png]]
> ![[LjoYkNQiVc.png]]
> ![[-pxA0pqWsT.png]]
> - Needs: support users to (1) identify things to critique (similar to the comparison features in [[ART - ChainForge]]); (2) translate feedback into principles (this happens under the hood; Joel Chan had mixed feelings about how effective it is); (3) test principles, which in Joel Chan's view is not well supported relative to [[ART - ChainForge]] or [[PTN - LLM Chains]].
