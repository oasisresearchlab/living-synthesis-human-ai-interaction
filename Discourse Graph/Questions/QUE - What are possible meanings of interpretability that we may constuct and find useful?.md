## Other Notes
Claude:
> The activity worked through a series of prompts. Most participants' slots were left blank in the notes; the answers recorded are S13's and the group's.
> - **Definition (S13):** a characteristic of a system's structure, mechanisms, and behaviors, such that they can be detailed from multiple viewpoints. This assumes the importance of triangulation and pluralism, instead of a singular truth.
> - **Structures** (what constructs are inside interpretability?): explanation, clarity.
> - **Functions** (how does it work?): "I ask a process for an explanation and I receive an intelligible response because the function (mechanism(s)) are simple."
> - **Behaviors** (what does it help us do?): trace a theory of process change from inputs to outputs, useful for explaining past model behavior and predicting future behavior. This assumes interpretability is possible and has a definitive "end" for specific cases. S13 asked: "Does interpretability have a definitive end goal or is it, like science, an endless quest?"
> - **Superordinate class:** value/usefulness. Interpretability + usefulness = value.
> - **Between interpretability and uninterpretability:** interpretability may be a continuous construct. How much interpretability do I need for context X?
> - **Why prioritize it?** It may not need to be complete for a model to be used fairly and effectively. It can help us understand whether the ideas underlying a model are worth expanding in future work, and understand our agency in using models (and whether we're giving it up "for a dystopian future").
> - **Related constructs from other literatures (S13):** triangulating arguments/justifications, explanation, theory, prediction, reliability, safety, decision-making / trust / explainability, transparency, fairness.
> - Left open: how interpretability came into existence and how it will change, and how ecologically valid this ontology is for scholars, decision-makers, and practitioners.
