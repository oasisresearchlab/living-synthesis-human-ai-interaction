## Context
Claude:
> Research question identified by S11 (👩🏽‍🔬 Distiller, SP-24) in [[@beede2020humancentered]]. Considerations surfaced by the fieldwork:
> - Eye-screening processes varied widely across 11 clinics, mostly because nurses had autonomy over how they organized the screening workflow, and resources differed by clinic (e.g., some clinics in Chiang Mai could use dilation drops, but none in Pathum Thani could).
> - Only two clinics had a dedicated screening room that could be darkened; the rest screened in shared spaces where lights could not be dimmed.
> - High volume: some clinics tried to screen 200 patients in 5 hours (90 seconds per patient), leading to extended hours or rescheduled patients.
> - Gradability: the system expected high-quality images (enough light reaching the back of the eye, large pupils, dark environment, dilation drops). 21% of initial images didn't meet its gradability standards, affected by non-darkened rooms and clinics not using dilation drops.
> - Nurses sometimes urged patients to follow through on referrals, and sometimes urged them not to, with evidence for why the model's output shouldn't be trusted, creating more burden for nurses.
> - Poor internet connections prevented results from coming back immediately.
