This site is a living synthesis of research on designing for human-AI interaction. It is structured as a [discourse graph](https://discoursegraphs.com): instead of summaries of papers, it's made of small, linked nodes, each one a question, a claim, a piece of evidence, or a pattern, artifact or theory that helps explain them.

It grew out of two PhD reading seminars on human-centered AI at the University of Maryland (Spring 2024 and Spring 2025), and it's meant to keep growing.

## Browse by node type

- [Questions](Discourse-Graph/Questions/): open research and design questions
- [Claims](Discourse-Graph/Claims/): generalized assertions that propose answers
- [Evidence](Discourse-Graph/Evidence/): specific observations from particular studies
- [Patterns](Discourse-Graph/Patterns/): design patterns and mechanisms abstracted from specific systems
- [Artifacts](Discourse-Graph/Artifacts/): specific systems and tools
- [Theories](Discourse-Graph/Theories/): theoretical frameworks
- [Sources](Discourse-Graph/Sources/): the papers behind it all

The graph view colors nodes by type, in the same colors as the list above uses in the vault: questions olive, claims green, evidence red, patterns bright green, artifacts pink, theories grey, sources light grey.

## What's reviewed and what isn't

Many nodes were extracted by Claude (an AI model) from seminar notes and recordings. These are marked **AI draft, not yet reviewed**, and listed in the [[AI draft review queue.base|review queue]]. Each one links back to the [extraction session](Extraction/) it came from. Treat them as leads until they've been reviewed.

Seminar participants are identified by pseudonyms (S01, S02, ...).
